English translation

And indeed, what we have already said would retain some validity even if we were to concede—which cannot be conceded without the gravest wickedness—that God does not exist, or that human affairs are not cared for by Him.

Syntactic analysis

Et haec quidem locum aliquem haberent
Main clause. Locum habere is an idiom meaning to hold valid or be applicable.
quae iam diximus
Relative clause modifying haec. Quae is accusative as the object of diximus.
etiamsi daremus
Concessive conditional clause. The imperfect subjunctives daremus and haberent express a present counterfactual hypothesis.
quod sine summo scelere dari nequit
Parenthetical relative clause. Quod refers to the hypothetical concession; nequit is in the present indicative, outside the counterfactual framework.
non esse Deum
First accusativus cum infinitivo (ACI): Deum is the subject of esse.
aut non curari ab eo negotia humana
Second ACI: negotia humana is the subject of the passive infinitive curari; ab eo expresses the personal agent.

Philosophical and theological significance

Grotius argues that the preceding principles of natural law would retain some validity even under the hypothetical denial of God's existence or providence. The parenthetical clause rejects that denial as gravely impious. The passage is often summarized by the formula etsi Deus non daretur, which is not its literal wording. The adjective aliquem qualifies the scope of the claim: the passage does not necessarily establish absolute independence from God.
